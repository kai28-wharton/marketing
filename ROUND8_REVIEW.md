# Getchell’s · final eight-round review

## Marketing verdict

**A late turnaround—not a fully completed 3+5 strategy.**

| Objective | Final R8 outcome | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Experience #1 | 6.6, rank #1 | Met, first achieved in R8 |
| Brand top two | 6.0, rank #1 | Met |
| Low Price at fair value | 4.7 versus 3.7 | Met |
| Frictionless at fair value | 3.9 versus 4.6 | Missed by 0.7 |

The Brand/Experience proposition succeeded in positioning. Service recovery from
1.5 at R6 to 3.0 at R8 complemented the improved shopping environment (3.3→3.6).
Execution was late and online capability fell 1.3→0.9. S5's convenience gate
remained unmet in every scored round.

S3+S5 finish with **$235.31M revenue**, **32.0%** of company revenue (−3.21 pp
versus R0), and **$520.84M aggregate CLV**, **28.1%** of customer equity
(−3.99 pp). Target revenue grew **18.7%** from R6 to R8, but contributed
**28.7%** of the R0→R8 revenue increase. S1 became the largest revenue and CLV
pool. Strong customer fit was not the same as target-segment dominance.

Lesson: protect service alongside Brand/ambience, enforce the S5 convenience
gate, track target mix as well as growth, and defend the substantial S1/S2/S6 base.
These are observed conclusions, not a segment-profit or causal ROI ranking.

## Customer segmentation: test the original priority order

The stated order is **S3 first → S5 second → supporting S2/S4/S6**. Growth
outside the targets is not itself a failure; compare fit, capability delivery and
commercial outcomes rather than assuming the targets must be the largest pools.

| Priority | Revenue growth R0→R8 | Revenue mix change | Interpretation |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| S3 first | 90.0% | −0.46 pp | Near company growth of 97.2%; retain the differentiated proposition, improve consistency of delivery |
| S5 second | 73.1% | −2.75 pp | 85.7% of target mix loss; Experience/convenience delivery was incomplete |
| S2 supporting | 68.2% | −3.21 pp | Weak defense; revenue fell 4.6% during R2–R6 |
| S4 supporting | 127.2% | +1.27 pp | Useful adjacent growth, not a demonstrated superior-margin target |
| S6 supporting | 112.9% | +1.36 pp | Important base growth; 19.9% of the company revenue increase |
| S1 outside priorities | 140.0% | +3.79 pp | Largest final revenue/CLV pool; investigate rather than retrospectively claim this as target-strategy success |

S2/S4/S6 supplied **46.2%** of revenue growth and **49.0%** of aggregate-CLV
growth; targets supplied **28.7% / 25.0%**. At unchanged R0 mix the targets
would represent $258.94M of actual R8 sales versus $235.31M observed. This
**$23.62M composition difference is not lost or recoverable revenue**.

Timing qualifies the late turnaround: target compound revenue growth per round
was **17.9% / 2.1% / 8.9%** over R0–R2 / R2–R6 / R6–R8, versus company
**22.7% / 2.6% / 8.8%**. Unequal windows are normalized per round; these are
descriptive phases, not identified treatment periods. Target mix stabilized
at 31.9%→32.0% late rather than recovering its starting weighting.

During R2–R6 service spending moved $14.00M→$13.08M while shopping-experience
spending rose $15.00M→$17.47M; service position fell 2.7→1.5 and Experience
score fell 5.8→4.8. During R6–R8 service spending rose to $29.00M, service
position recovered to 3.0 and Experience reached 6.6. Online spending fell
$16.00M→$10.00M and online position 1.3→0.9 despite analytics rising
$13.00M→$16.94M. Analytics is a multiplier, not standalone service demand or a
replacement for online delivery. Store contraction/restoration (236→190→250)
and higher-cost breadth changes (50→40→50) warrant separate scrutiny for S2.

The page plots mix changes, portfolio contributions, normalized phase growth,
spending and capabilities separately. Multiple inputs and peers changed together;
these observations do not identify segment-specific causal effects or spending
returns. Keep S3's differentiation, enforce S5's convenience safeguard, distinguish
S2 defense from S4/S6 growth and explicitly recognize S1. Segment margins,
retention and acquisition costs are needed before claiming an optimal ranking.

## Finance

Market value **$93.20M→$1,038.04M**; net income **−$6.29M→$75.83M**.
EBIT ends at **$127.59M**, gross margin **40.5%**, debt **$162.41M**.
Debt is below R0 but rose **$42.66M** after R6 as stores expanded **190→250**.
WACC ends at **10.0%**, versus **7.8%** initially; value recovery was not a
falling-WACC story. Final unit share **12.3%** remains below R0's **13.0%**.
