Getchell’s is a century-old department store with a rich history, dating back to its pioneering namesake – the first female executive in American business, whose personal motto was “be everywhere, do everything and never fail to astonish the customer.” This ideology launched the company from small storefront to iconic retail giant and set the model for department stores across the world.

Today, Getchell’s star doesn’t shine quite as bright, having been eclipsed by strong competition from both the higher-end luxury side of the shopping spectrum as well as the lower-priced “fast fashion” end. Nevertheless, it remains one of the nation’s most recognizable retailers and a mainstay anchor-store at hundreds of regional shopping malls. But with indoor retail meccas losing out to more convenient buying experiences, and traditional anchors like KTBenney shuttering stores as foot traffic fades, Getchell’s must find a way to maintain its relevance in an evolving marketplace.

With annual revenue still consistently bolstered by the holiday season, a fairly strong omni-channel presence, and a versatile product portfolio featuring everything from designer labels to bargain brands, Getchell’s has been able to maintain its competitive foothold. But it does suffer from a low inventory turnover – a negative attribute that has persistently dragged down the in-store shopping experience it was once famous for. This has led company executives to seek out fresh approaches for meeting the modern customer’s needs and preferences, while re-establishing a strong market position.
